# Literature-gap verification — Project 1

## Working question

Has an India-wide forest-fire occurrence classification study used the specific combination of:

- VIIRS Suomi-NPP FIRMS observations;
- India-wide coverage;
- 2018–2025 observation period;
- historical meteorological variables matched to fire/non-fire observations; and
- multi-timescale weather-derived features such as 24 h, 72 h, and 168 h precipitation?

## Evidence found so far

### Sharma, Gupta & Fatima (2022)

The study evaluates six machine-learning algorithms for Indian forest-fire susceptibility using MODIS fire hotspots from 2001–2020 and forest, climatic, and topographic predictors. It reports ROC-AUC values of 0.908 for SVM and 0.903 for ANN.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1071/WF22016

### AutoML-Fire (2025)

A pan-India study uses a dataset spanning 2003–2018 and combines weather and socio-economic/environmental variables. It uses automated machine learning, SHAP, partial dependence analysis, uncertainty analysis, and spatial analysis.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2025.106578

## Preliminary conclusion

The broad idea of machine-learning-based Indian forest-fire prediction is **not novel**. A novelty claim based only on “using machine learning for Indian forest fires” is therefore not defensible.

The 2018–2025 VIIRS FIRMS period may still contribute to a narrower research gap, but this requires a systematic search for the exact combination of satellite product, temporal coverage, meteorological integration, target construction, and validation protocol.

## Required next search

Search combinations should include:

- India + VIIRS + FIRMS + forest fire prediction
- India + SNPP VIIRS + meteorological + fire occurrence

The final novelty statement must be written only after this verification.
